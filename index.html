How a project unfolds

Six stages. One team, start to finish.

This is roughly the order a real project moves through — though the work doesn't stop being connected once it starts. Tap a stage to see what it covers.

Before access can be secured, we need to know who owns the land, who holds the mineral interests, and who has the authority to grant the rights a project requires.

APS researches recorded deeds, conveyances, mineral interests, easements, and other public records to establish ownership and trace the chain of title. When records aren't available online or the answer requires a closer look, we go directly to county courthouses and other public offices to find and review the underlying documents.

Getting it right here prevents problems everywhere else.

Our work can include

  • Surface and mineral ownership research
  • Deed and public-record research
  • Courthouse research and document retrieval
  • Chain-of-title research
  • Full title search back to sovereignty
  • Mineral rights title opinions
  • Surface and land ownership title opinions
  • Ownership research for mineral permitting
  • Identification of gaps and conflicting records
  • Title curative

Once the land and ownership are understood, that information needs to remain accurate, organized, and useful.

APS builds and maintains project maps and databases that bring surface and mineral ownership, topography, infrastructure, project boundaries, and other project information together. Because our GIS work is handled alongside the land work behind it, the people researching, permitting, managing, and working in the field can rely on the same information.

Good decisions start with good information. We keep it accurate, organized, and usable.

GIS services can include

  • Surface and mineral ownership mapping
  • Topographic and infrastructure mapping
  • Project maps and exhibits
  • Project databases
  • Ownership and tract information
  • Ongoing mapping and data coordination

Permitting has been at the center of APS's work since 1980. We've successfully permitted thousands of square miles across 23 states, including difficult areas and projects brought to us after the work had already become complicated.

APS identifies the appropriate landowners and mineral owners, makes direct contact, explains the proposed survey work, negotiates the necessary permits, and keeps the client informed as the project moves forward. We also coordinate with municipal, county, state, federal, and tribal entities when required.

An experienced APS manager remains directly involved throughout the work. Clients know where the project stands, and landowners know who they're dealing with.

Permitting services can include

  • Surface and mineral permitting
  • Landowner contact and negotiation
  • Government and agency coordination
  • Permit tracking and reporting
  • Landowner payment processing and delivery
  • Check generation and tracking
  • 1099 preparation and required IRS filings
  • Coordination with project and field personnel

Right-of-way work starts with many of the same fundamentals: accurate ownership, direct communication, clear terms, and agreements that reflect what the project actually requires.

APS works directly with property owners to explain the proposed use, negotiate applicable terms, secure the necessary agreements, and keep the documentation organized as acquisition progresses. Our experience in title, GIS, permitting, and field operations means we're looking beyond the signature to what the agreement needs to accomplish on the ground.

Every agreement involves real property and real people. We treat both accordingly.

Right-of-way services can include

  • Ownership verification
  • Landowner contact
  • Easement and access negotiations
  • Agreement acquisition
  • Payment coordination
  • Agency coordination
  • Documentation and reporting
  • Ongoing project support

A signed permit or agreement isn't the end of our involvement. Once work moves into the field, the conditions agreed to on paper still have to be understood and followed.

APS helps keep clients, landowners, agents, GIS personnel, and field crews working from the same information. Our project managers provide direct oversight, maintain reporting, respond as conditions change, and help ensure field activity remains consistent with the permits, agreements, and project requirements already in place.

We stay involved because what happens in the field matters just as much as what was agreed before it got there.

This can include

  • Direct project oversight
  • Client reporting and communication
  • Landowner coordination
  • Permit and agreement compliance
  • Coordination with field personnel
  • Resolving issues as they arise
